# PART I — THE HABIT ENGINE THESIS

## The key realization

An exceptional product is not one that contains a lot of information. It is one that **removes a recurring piece of friction from the user's life**.

Right now Investor/Pass is very strong as a **research library**. To become something people use eagerly and repeatedly, it needs to become a **research habit**.

Adjacent-market products (Quartr, AlphaSense) are built around global search, transcript search, source traceability, watchlists, alerts, recaps, highlighting, exports, monitoring, workflow automation, citations and deep research. We borrow the **behavioral lessons**, not copy the products.

## The real question

Not: *"What other feature can we add?"*

Instead: **"What would make a serious investor feel slightly handicapped without Investor/Pass?"**

The answer: **Investor/Pass should become the place where a person continuously tracks, studies, compares and remembers investor thinking.**

## The Investor/Pass Habit Loop

```text
DISCOVER
```

That **FOLLOW + WHAT'S NEW** layer is the missing piece.

## 1. FOLLOW — a core feature

A user can follow: an **investor**, a **theme**, a **company**, an **event/topic**, a **saved search**.

Example follows: Warren Buffett · Economic Moats · Coca-Cola · Inflation.

Then Investor/Pass knows: *"These are the things this user cares about."* No AI required — simply structured user preference.

## 2. "What's New Since You Last Visited?"

One of the highest-retention features. A Pro user returns after five days and sees:

> # SINCE YOUR LAST VISIT
> **7 new sources indexed**
> **3 new Buffett references**
> **2 new Marks references**
> **1 new source connected to "Risk"**
> **1 new company relationship**
>
> ### Continue where you left off
> Buffett → Inflation → 2008

This changes the product from **a website** into **a place that keeps your research alive.**

## 3. WATCHLISTS — but for ideas, not stocks

Called **FOLLOWING** or **MY WATCHLIST**:

### Investors
Buffett · Munger · Marks
### Ideas
Risk · Inflation · Moats
### Companies
Coca-Cola · Apple
### Events
Financial crises

Then: **"Your watchlist has 14 new references this week."** Each row clickable, each row summarizing activity (`BUFFETT — 7 new references`).

Adapted to **investor thinking**, not live stock prices.

## 4. INTELLIGENT ALERTS — before AI

No AI required. A user follows Howard Marks; whenever a new legitimate source is added: **"New Howard Marks source."** Follows Inflation: **"12 new inflation references."** Follows Buffett + Coca-Cola: **"New Buffett/Coca-Cola references."**

Delivery: email · in-product notification · weekly digest. Entirely deterministic.

## 5. THE WEEKLY INVESTOR BRIEF

Not a traditional newsletter — a **personalized product notification**:

> # YOUR WEEK IN INVESTOR/PASS
> ### You follow
> Buffett · Marks · Inflation · Coca-Cola
> ### This week
> **9 new sources** · **14 new references** · **2 new relationships**
> ### Worth exploring
> Howard Marks — market cycles · Buffett — capital allocation · Buffett × Munger — business quality
>
> **OPEN YOUR LIBRARY →**

Retention infrastructure. Later, once AI arrives, this becomes an AI-generated research brief.

## 6. RESEARCH CONTINUITY

Every research session resumable. Yesterday's path:

```text
Buffett → Inflation → 1987 → Coca-Cola → Capital Allocation
```

Today the product says: **CONTINUE YOUR RESEARCH — Buffett → Inflation — Last opened: 1987 Shareholder Letter — [CONTINUE]**

People hate losing research context.

## 7. THE RESEARCH SESSION

When someone begins deep research, track it automatically:

> # RESEARCH SESSION — **Buffett on inflation**
> Opened: 6 sources · 3 themes · 2 companies · 1 event
>
> **[SAVE SESSION]** → Collection

Browsing becomes structured research activity.

## 8. IDEA EVOLUTION — first-class product

# BUFFETT — INFLATION
- **1970–1980** — Early thinking
- **1980–1990** — Refinement
- **1990–2000** — Expansion
- **2000–2010** — Crisis context
- **2010–2025** — Later view

Plus: sources per period, companies, events, terminology changes, related ideas.

Not just a timeline — a **thought-evolution engine**. Deterministic. No AI needed.

Do NOT call these "changed his mind" unless evidence establishes actual change. Use: *Early references · Later references · Recurring themes · Different contexts.*

## 9. THEN VS NOW — signature feature

For any investor/theme: Early Buffett vs Later Buffett. Historical sources, themes, terminology, company examples, decisions. Answers the sophisticated reader's key question: **"Did they actually change their mind?"**

## 10. REPEATED IDEAS

If Buffett discusses a concept across 1977, 1985, 1995, 2008, 2018, surface: **"This concept appears across 5 decades."**

> # CAPITAL ALLOCATION
> Referenced in 112 sources · First indexed: 1977 · Most recent indexed: 2025

Frequency as discovery mechanism. Only actual database counts. Never fabricated metrics.

## 11. WHO TALKS ABOUT THIS?

Search "pricing power" →

> 1. Buffett — 72 references
> 2. Fisher — 41
> 3. Munger — 28
> 4. Terry Smith — 24
> 5. Lynch — 19
>
> [COMPARE THEIR THINKING →]

Counts are **indexed reference counts**, not investment-quality rankings. Search becomes discovery.

## 12. WHO AGREES?

Deterministic overlap model, not philosophical judgment:

- **Strong source overlap**: Buffett, Munger, Fisher, Terry Smith
- **Related concepts**: Lynch, Greenblatt
- **Different emphasis**: Bogle, Dalio

Defined by measurable metadata overlap: shared canonical concepts, themes, companies, events. Documented in codebase.

## 13. WHO DISAGREES? → "DIFFERING EMPHASIS"

For DIVERSIFICATION: compare Buffett, Munger, Bogle, Graham via evidence-linked perspectives. Never assert disagreement unless the corpus establishes it. Prefer: *Different emphasis · Concentration-oriented sources · Diversification-oriented sources.* Every classification evidence-based.

## 14. EVIDENCE MATRIX — signature feature

20 investors × important ideas:

| Investor | Risk | Moats | Inflation | Valuation | Management | Diversification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buffett | ● | ● | ● | ● | ● | ● |
| Munger | ● | ● |   | ● | ● | ● |

Every dot clickable → `Buffett × Inflation → 37 indexed references, 18 sources, 1970–2025`.

The dot means **sufficient verified indexed coverage exists** — NOT that the investor endorses the concept. Legend/tooltips required.

## 15. INVESTOR DEPTH = COVERAGE SCORE

Never "Buffett = 98/100 investor." Measure **coverage**, not quality:

> Warren Buffett — Sources: 1,248 · Themes: 38 · Companies: 217 · Events: 62 · Years covered: 50+
> Howard Marks — Sources: 421 · Themes: 31 · Companies: 81 · Events: 24 · Years covered: 30+

## 16. SOURCE QUALITY / PROVENANCE

Source class: Primary · Institutional · Interview · Secondary · Historical · Public Domain · Licensed.
Verification state: Verified · Needs Review. Only mark verified when provenance rules actually passed.

## 17. SOURCE TYPE EXPLORER

Browse/filter: Shareholder Letters · Investor Memos · Interviews · Speeches · Books · Annual Reports · SEC Filings · Presentations · Academic Papers · Media Interviews. Metadata-driven — no hard-coded frontend lists that drift from the database.

## 18. THE SOURCE STACK

## 19. DECISION LEDGER — biggest differentiator

# DECISIONS

Buffett:
```text

1991  SALOMON BROTHERS INTERVENTION             Context · Source · Outcome

```

Icahn: stake · activist campaign · board action · settlement · exit.

Do not force identical behavior where it makes no sense. Shared core schema + investor-specific metadata.

This creates **"What did they actually do?"** rather than only *"What did they say?"*

## 20. STATEMENT → DECISION → OUTCOME

Signature historical workflow:

```text
INVESTOR → STATEMENT → DECISION → COMPANY/EVENT → OUTCOME
```

Every link points to evidence. Never infer an outcome if unknown. Supported states: **KNOWN · PARTIAL · UNKNOWN**.

## 21. COMPANY INVESTOR HISTORY

APPLE page shows per-investor history: first reference → investment → increased → reduced → current status. Plus themes (Capital allocation, Brand, Moat, Technology) and sources. Companies become entry points into investor research. Huge SEO surface.

## 22. WHAT DID THEY DO?

Company pages organize records: **SAID** (statements) · **BOUGHT** (investment) · **HELD** (period) · **SOLD** (exit) · **WHY** (only when source-backed). Do not infer reasons without evidence.

# PART III — THE BLOW-AWAY LAYER

Follow + New + Personal Home + Decision Ledger + the rest of the habit layer puts the product in a different category. That is necessary, not sufficient. This layer sits on top and makes a serious consumer feel **blown away**.

## The real bar

A sophisticated user should finish a 12–20 minute session thinking:

> "I just did in one place what normally takes me 10 tabs, a notes app, and half a Saturday — and the system already knows what I care about."

Or shorter: **"I don't want to research investor thinking anywhere else."**

Produced by a small number of *moments*, not feature count.

## The 9 moments

**1. The first search result (0–20s).** Denser and more trustworthy than Google + PDFs + Notion. Investor + year + source title instantly. One-line "why this matters". Entity chips. Provenance path. "Why you're seeing this" proving structured retrieval. Immediate actions: Open source · Context · Follow · Save · Compare. If the first result feels thin, the session is lost.

**2. Graph traversal (1–4 min).** Investor → Theme → Company → Source → Decision → related Theme without dead ends. High-signal next links everywhere. Honest clickable counts. Continuous back/continue. Where the product stops feeling like a website.

**3. The "what they did" moment.** Statement → Decision → Context → Source → Outcome one click away while reading any company/event/theme. Most products give the quote; we give the historical action. Different category.

**4. Follow → ownership.** Instant confirmation the system now has a preference model. Personal Home updates. Clear promise of new material. Zero friction to follow from any entity page. Emotional shift from browsing to mine.

**5. The return moment ("New since last visit").** Highest-leverage retention moment. Exact counts, exact matched entities, one click into new material, "continue where you left off" beside it. Precise and calm even when corpus is small — never empty, never salesy.

**6. Compare moment.** Shared/distinct ideas, language, historical context, sources. No unsupported judgments. Easy drill into evidence. Saveable comparison. Proves the product is about *thinking*, not retrieval.

**7. Save → Library.** Building a personal research instrument, not bookmarking. Instant, reversible. Collections as real research projects. Saved searches restore full context. Dense and utilitarian.

**8. Pro unlock.** Next page of the *same* research session, not a different product. Specific locked counts ("14 more references"). Return to exact state post-payment. Continuity features available without reorientation.

**9. "I could stay here for an hour."** Density and relatedness on Theme pages, Investor timeline, Company history, Decision Ledger. Editorial + structural quality, not more chrome.

## Precision moves (on top of shipping habit features)

**A. Real density on 6 surfaces** — search results, passage/context, investor page, theme page, company page, personal home/library. Each answers: Where am I? What am I looking at? Where can I go next? Any thin surface makes the whole product thin.

**B. Over-invest in Decisions** — best 10–15 Buffett decisions populated with care; excellent chain UI. More category differentiation than five secondary features.

**C. Script the first 12 minutes** — 0–1 search works · 1–3 result+context+provenance · 3–5 graph move · 5–7 decision · 7–9 follow+save · 9–12 home/"we'll surface new material". Remove every friction point.

**D. Honest "New"** — "1 new source related to Capital Allocation since your last visit" beats inflated or empty states. Precision builds trust.

**E. Kill dead ends and generic empty states** — no-next-action page = architecture failure; "No results"-only empty state = product failure.

**F. Visible Pro value pre-paywall** — counts, previews, graph glimpses make locked depth a natural continuation.

**G. Performance and calm** — instant search/navigation, motion only for state, zero layout shift, keyboard-first where it matters.

## Psychological sequence being sold

1. Surprise at structure → 2. Trust from provenance → 3. Pull from the graph → 4. Ownership from Follow/Save → 5. Anticipation of return → 6. Willingness to pay ($19 obvious).

Optimize 1–3 → admiration. Deliver 4–6 → dependence.

## Done looks like

Search works on real questions · frictionless graph movement · said AND done visible · topics followed · precise return value · continuous saveable research · felt Pro boundary. When that loop is tight, **the software is the moat**.

## Final directive

Past adding conceptual features. Now **compressing excellence into the critical path**:

1. Finish Follow + New + Personal Home + Decision Ledger cleanly.
2. Spend remaining energy exclusively on the 9 moments.
3. Test the first 12 minutes on a clean browser as a skeptical, time-poor analyst.
4. Fix everything thin, slow, generic, discontinuous.
5. Make Decision and graph-traversal moments feel like a different category.

Execute against the moments, not the backlog.
